Hole World combos and bonus time: how to score more before the clock runs out

In Mindora Hole World, swallowing more objects is not the only way to improve a run. Collecting them close together can multiply your points and earn valuable seconds. That changes how you should plan a route: a nearby chain of small objects may be more useful than one impressive object far away.

The short version

Collect the next object less than 4.2 seconds after the previous collection finishes to extend your combo. Every fifth item in a continuous combo awards five extra seconds, subject to the timer cap. The points multiplier increases with the combo, but is capped at 5×.

1. What counts as a combo?

The game checks the elapsed time between completed collections. If the next object finishes collecting within the 4.2-second window, your combo increases by one. Otherwise it resets to one. The displayed combo therefore rewards a steady chain, not simply how many objects you have collected in the whole level.

Example: imagine you collect objects at 10.0, 12.0, 15.5 and 20.0 seconds. The first three form a combo of 1, 2 and 3 because the gaps are 2.0 and 3.5 seconds. The last gap is 4.5 seconds, so the fourth collection starts a new combo of 1. This is an illustrative timeline, not a recorded play session.

2. How combo points are calculated

For each completed collection, the game starts with 10 points. Objects with an internal size value above 1 add 15 base points. That base is multiplied by the current combo, up to a maximum multiplier of five.

Formula: points for a collection = (10 + 15 if the object's size value is above 1) × min(current combo, 5).

This hypothetical chain earns 210 points. The size threshold is a game-code value, not necessarily the visual width of an object. After reaching five, further uninterrupted collections continue to use the 5× scoring cap.

3. The five-item bonus can save your run

When the combo reaches 5, 10, 15 and so on, Hole World adds five seconds to the remaining timer. However, remaining time cannot exceed the round's configured starting time plus 25 seconds. This is a cap on stored time, not a promise that every bonus will give the full five seconds if you are already near the cap.

Example: with 18 seconds remaining, completing a five-item combo increases the clock to 23 seconds. But if a round starts with 145 seconds and your clock somehow already reads 169 seconds, the same bonus can only take it to 170 seconds, not 174.

4. Easy, Medium or Hard: what actually changes?

At level one, the game uses the following settings. Higher levels increase both the required collection count and the time allowance, so these are level-one values rather than universal settings.

Each later level adds up to 12 required items (three per level) and up to 30 seconds (five per level). Difficulty also affects movement speed in the game code. Choosing Easy is useful for learning the map; Hard asks you to collect more in less time.

5. A better route: clusters before distance

Suppose you see four small objects together near your portal and one larger object across an open road. A long trip to the larger object may break your combo. Collecting the four nearby objects first can build the multiplier, then you can decide whether crossing the road is worth the travel time. The exact answer depends on object positions and your current portal size.

Use this three-step route check:

  1. Find a cluster: identify two or more objects that your current portal can already swallow.
  2. Keep the chain moving: steer toward the next reachable object before the 4.2-second combo window expires.
  3. Reassess after growth: once the portal expands, look for previously inaccessible objects near your current route rather than automatically crossing the entire map.

For portal growth, steering and object-size fundamentals, read our earlier Hole World route strategy. This article focuses on scoring and timer mechanics rather than repeating those basics.

6. Try a practical three-round experiment

Round A: Learn the route

Play on Easy. Focus on finishing a level and noting where the densest small-object clusters appear. Ignore your points for now.

Round B: Build one five-item chain

Play Easy again. Try to collect five objects with each gap below 4.2 seconds. Watch for the +5-second bonus and compare your best combo with Round A.

Round C: Compare on the same difficulty

Repeat Easy once more and deliberately prioritize nearby chains. Compare final points, best combo and whether you completed the level. Avoid comparing an Easy score with a Hard score as though the starting conditions were identical.

Quick self-check

Question: You collect five small objects without breaking the combo. How many points do those five collections earn, and what timer reward is triggered?

Answer: 10 + 20 + 30 + 40 + 50 = 150 points, plus a five-second timer bonus at combo 5, limited by the round's timer cap.
